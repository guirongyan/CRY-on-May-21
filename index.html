About

Cornerstones for Resilience - Youth is a youth-led national network connecting the dots of hazard mitigation, public education, house appraisal, insurance incentives, and resilience finance to improve disaster resilience.

To all youths: my future, I am in charge.

Vision

Laying cornerstones for community resilience by empowering today's youth to establish a systemic resilience mindset and conduct resilience education.

Mission

The vision will be fulfilled through establishing a national youth network to dance, sing, speak, convene and finance for resilience.

01

Age-Appropriate Education Programs

Develop and deliver age-appropriate hazard awareness and preparedness education programs and materials for K-12 students and the public, reviewed and approved by advisory boards composed of subject-matter experts and educators.

02

Youth Resilience & Public Speaking Training

Training youth nationwide in resilience knowledge and public speaking skills to effectively communicate disaster risk reduction and preparedness strategies, producing Resilience Ambassadors nationwide.

03

Resilience Education & Outreach

Nurturing the young generation for resilience by dancing and singing; increasing hazard risk awareness of the public and sharing hazard mitigation strategies using presentations.

04

Incentivizing Resilience via House Appraisal Change

Convening interagency forums to promote integrating resilience features into property appraisal, tailored to hazards such as tornadoes, hurricanes, floods, wildfires, and earthquakes — including researchers, policymakers, emergency managers, housing professionals, and community leaders.

05

Involving the Insurance Industry

Help researchers disseminate their most recent research to the insurance industry, expediting adoption of new secondary features related to resilience, leading to lower insurance premiums.

06

Resilience Finance (Resilience Bonds)

Money talks. Promoting innovative funding mechanisms such as resilience bonds to enable communities to take resilience actions prior to hazards — preventing hazards from becoming disasters.

Roadmap

Strategic Plan

Year 1

50 Resilience Ambassadors for one hazard (tornadoes) in Missouri, Oklahoma, Ohio, and Texas.

Year 2

50 Resilience Ambassadors for each hazard in multiple states across tornadoes, flooding, hurricanes, and wildfire — leading to 200 members.

Year 3

50 Resilience Ambassadors will be advanced to Resilience Fellows.

Year 4

100 Resilience Ambassadors for each hazard — leading to 400 members.

Accountability

Measurable Impact

  • Number of hazard-awareness education programs/materials developed
  • Number of resilience ambassadors trained
  • Number of presentations delivered by resilience ambassadors
  • Number of schools, summer camps, and communities reached
  • Number of interagency meetings convened
  • Number of travel awards
  • Amount of funds raised for disaster recoveries, travel funds, or cash awards

Membership

Why Join

Benefits of Members

  • Get training in summer/winter breaks on public speaking for the topics of resilience.
  • Improve leadership skills.
  • Establish systemic thinking for resilience.
  • Year-round, accumulate resilience knowledge from hazard experts through Zoom meetings.
  • Create opportunities for members to do TED talks.
  • Annual conference in various locations.
  • Annual Resilience Competition (education or research). Winners obtain partial or full travel support to conferences like the AGU Annual Conference in Washington DC, AMS Annual Meetings, the Annual Natural Hazards Workshop in Denver, the Global Alliance of Disaster Research Institutes Annual Meetings in Japan, or UNESCO Meetings in Switzerland. The number of awards depends on the fundraising situation.
  • Members will be qualified to be evaluated to become Fellows after 2 years of commitment.
  • Get volunteer hours.
  • Get a shirt with the organization logo.
  • Earn a cash reward for each outreach activity — e.g., in schools, summer camps, local libraries, churches, local museums, local science centers, local governments, community organizations, and public events — after passing the presenting skill evaluation.
